## 1. Endpoint and provenance

- **Records:** 144 complete records (of 279 rows in Supplementary Data 3; the other 135 rows carry only inhibitor and mutation names): 8 inhibitors × 31 mutations at 20 positions (sparse: 144 of 248 possible pairs).
- **Quantity:** ΔΔG = RT ln(IC50_mut/IC50_WT) at 300 K, positive = resistance, derived by the authors under a competitive Michaelis-Menten assumption with K_M ≫ [ATP]; the paper itself lists unmodelled factors (drug metabolism, expression, feedback). It is not a binding free energy. Resistant = ΔΔG ≥ 1.36 kcal/mol (10-fold) in both papers.
- **Reproducibility of ΔΔG from IC50:** for every inhibitor the sheet's ΔΔG equals RT ln(IC50/IC50_WT,implied) (maximum residual 0.014 kcal/mol for seven inhibitors), with one exception: **ponatinib G250E** has IC50 0.021 nM but ΔΔG 0.3 kcal/mol, whereas that IC50 would imply -2.96. The inconsistency is unresolved and left uncorrected.
- **Censoring:** 2 records have IC50 = 10,000 nM (dasatinib T315I, imatinib L248R), described in the paper as above the assay range; the sheet stores them as equalities, so their ΔΔG values (5.08, 2.33) are lower bounds. **Resolution:** IC50 is integer nM; extra rows sharing an IC50 value within an inhibitor: {'axitinib': 0, 'bosutinib': 0, 'dasatinib': 7, 'erlotinib': 0, 'gefitinib': 0, 'imatinib': 1, 'nilotinib': 2, 'ponatinib': 11}. No per-record replicate or uncertainty exists; the authors' variability estimates are inter-laboratory (0.36 kcal/mol, 97 ΔΔGs) and ΔIC50 against ΔKd (0.81 kcal/mol, 27 mutations).
- **Assay mix (not one assay):** per the paper, 131 records are cell-viability IC50 values; the 13 erlotinib and gefitinib records come from Davis et al. 2011 and are binding constants (the paper's Fig. 2d calls ref. 82 ΔKd). In this intake 4 of 7 erlotinib and 3 of 6 gefitinib values equal the phosphorylated-Abl block of that sheet and none equals the non-phosphorylated block; the remaining records are not in the sheet, so their block is untested. The structural models are non-phosphorylated.
- **Source tables:** Schrock 2013 is a conference abstract (Blood 122, 3992), so five of the eight inhibitors trace to an abstract; the three source tables were not downloaded or verified. ## 2. Units and what can be generalized

Unique records: 144; substitutions: 31; residue positions: 20 (1 to 5 substitutions each; positions 315, 317, 359 carry three or more); inhibitors: 8; assay families: 2 (cell viability, binding); source studies: 3; papers using the table: 2 (not independent). Resistant records: 19, spread over 10 substitutions at 6 positions (248, 253, 255, 299, 315, 317) and 5 inhibitors (none for axitinib, ponatinib or gefitinib).

- **Realistic generalization:** new residue positions within Abl (grouped by position), or new inhibitors (leave-one-inhibitor-out, as in Aldeghi's ML2). Repeated inhibitors for one mutation are not independent mutations. Cross-kinase claims are not testable from this Abl-only set.
- **Precision:** with 6 resistant-containing positions and 14 without, the Hanley-McNeil standard error of a site-level AUROC at a true value of 0.8 is 0.12 (a formula, not a result). Only a large improvement could be detected; resistant/susceptible classification is descriptive only.

- **Deterministic sample:** all eight models (the whole input set; no sampling), covering both construction routes. Chain rule: the chain with the most standard residues (the authors' own choice). Alternative water/no-water files give identical protein Cα for five inhibitors and differ by up to 0.10 Å for the docked pair.
- **Cα comparison (rigid Kabsch, 270 common residues 233-502):** the eight traces fall in two families (the conformational state of either family was not assessed here): axitinib, bosutinib, dasatinib, erlotinib, gefitinib 0.014-1.30 Å apart, and imatinib, nilotinib, ponatinib 0.70-1.35 Å apart, with 5.0-5.3 Å between families. The erlotinib and gefitinib models differ by 0.014 Å, about 14 times the 0.001 Å floor set by three-decimal PDB coordinates and far below any structural change of interest: the same receptor. **Consequences:** (i) Cα geometry varies with the inhibitor template, never with the mutation, so there is no mutation-specific backbone information and a Cα-change predictor is undefined here; (ii) a template-level Cα feature is a function of inhibitor identity (a baseline); (iii) the Cα trace cannot separate erlotinib from gefitinib.
- **Other legitimate task with existing inputs:** the wild-type complexes support site-context features: ligand-site distance (3-6 of 20 positions are within 4.5 Å of the ligand, and 5-11 lie beyond 10 Å, so distal positions matter), side-chain contacts, and local backbone geometry of the template at the position. No existing TopoFold component computes mutation-aware contact or burial features.

## 4. Study design (decision B)

- **Question:** for new Abl residue positions, does any structure-derived site descriptor of the matched wild-type complex (side-chain contacts, ligand distance, TopoFold Cα κ/τ window) improve the ranking of variant-drug ΔΔG over mutation-property and inhibitor baselines?
- **Endpoint and units:** ΔΔG_exp (kcal/mol, positive = resistance) as supplied; the two censored records flagged as lower bounds and excluded from the primary regression metric; the ponatinib G250E row excluded and reported; resistant (≥ 1.36) AUPRC descriptive only.
- **Inputs:** the 144 records, the eight wild-type templates, and P00519 numbering. **Strongest baselines (equal information):** inhibitor mean ΔΔG from training positions; mutation properties (BLOSUM62, volume, hydrophobicity, charge change); ligand distance and contact counts at the position; their combination in one regularized model. TopoFold features enter only as an added block.
- **Grouping and holdout:** leave-one-position-out over 20 positions (all substitutions and inhibitors at a position held out together); secondary leave-one-inhibitor-out. Scaling and any selection inside each training fold.
- **Uncertainty:** bootstrap over positions; with this few positions a descriptive or insufficient-evidence outcome is the expected result unless the improvement is large. Minimum useful improvement to be frozen before any scoring (a margin in RMSE and rank correlation against the strongest baseline), with the equivalence question stated separately.
- **Leakage controls:** the template of the held-out inhibitor is its own; no label, hotspot list or resistant-site knowledge enters any feature; the inspected sample is development material (below); features and thresholds fixed before scoring.
- **Smallest next implementation:** a deterministic feature-table builder (no model) that reads the eight templates and the 144 records and writes site-context and mutation-property features with unit tests (numbering, wild-type identity, distances against an independent calculation); then freeze the protocol.
